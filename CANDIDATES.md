# Candidate lines — judged against the published judge brief

Judge criteria, most important first:
1. Spoken by the machine itself, as a pickup line a vending machine
   would use on a passing human (a line any stranger in a bar could
   say loses).
2. Uses the machine's own world: slot, buttons, coils, snacks, hum,
   exact change.
3. Gets a laugh, punch arriving in the last few words.
4. Short and speakable, one line read in a breath.
5. Charming rather than creepy.

## The ten candidates

1. (132) "Hey — I'm the machine in the corner, and I've been holding
   this Snickers for someone exactly like you. Press B4, it's already
   yours." — Machine voice and slot code are there, but the punch
   ("it's already yours") is flat; reads like an ad, not a joke.
2. (117) "I've dropped snacks for a hundred strangers, but my coil's
   never spun this fast for anyone. Want to see what B7 does?" —
   Good coil image, but the ending is a shrug, not a punch.
3. (102) "You don't need exact change to get my attention — just press
   E5 and I'll drop something sweet for you." — "Exact change" is
   pure machine world, but "something sweet" is a generic punch.
4. (126) "I've been humming the same tune for three years, but you
   walked by and my buttons lit up. Press C2 — I saved you the last
   one." — Warm and machine-voiced, but the ending is sweet rather
   than funny.
5. (107) "Most people just want my chips. You looked at me like I was
   the snack. Press A1 — first one's on the house." — Strong turn
   ("I was the snack") and a good punch; close contender.
6. (96) "My coils only turn for coins, but I'd jam myself just to keep
   you standing here a little longer." — Vivid and funny: a machine
   deliberately jamming is original. Slightly needy, second place.
7. (112) "I have forty snacks and one working light, but you're the
   only thing in this hallway worth staring at. Press D3." — Good
   setup, but the punch ("worth staring at") lands mid-line and the
   trailing "Press D3" dilutes it.
8. (116) "You walk past every day and my display still says SOLD OUT —
   because the only thing I want to dispense is my number." —
   **WINNER.** First-person machine; display, SOLD OUT and dispensing
   are all machine world; the punch ("dispense is my number") lands in
   the last few words; 116 characters, one breath; cheeky and warm.
9. (112) "I've got cold drinks, warm chips, and one empty coil with
   your name on it. Come closer — B6 is blinking for you." — "Empty
   coil with your name on it" is lovely, but "blinking for you" is a
   soft ending.
10. (101) "My whole love language is: insert coin, press a button,
    drop a snack. But for you, I'd skip the coin." — Great machine
    vocabulary, but "skip the coin" is a shrug, not a laugh.

## Picked

Candidate 8 — it is the only one that scores on all five criteria at
once, and its punch is the cleanest wordplay of the ten: the machine
does the one thing it was built to do (dispense) with the one thing a
pickup line is for (its number).
